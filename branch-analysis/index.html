Branch Operations — Process Improvement Analysis

A business analyst case study: diagnosing service bottlenecks across a retail branch network, redesigning the customer service process, and quantifying the impact — in time, cost, and satisfaction.

Illustrative case · sample figures

1 · The problem

Branch transaction data showed rising wait times and falling satisfaction, concentrated in routine counter transactions that could be served digitally.

Avg. wait time: 14 minTarget ≤ 8 min · worsening 3 quarters in a row
Counter transactions: 58%Routine deposits & withdrawals still handled in person
Staff utilization: 91%Above the 85% healthy threshold — burnout risk
Digital adoption: 42%Growing, but well below the 65% target

2 · Process redesign: as-is → to-be

Mapping the current customer journey exposed three handoffs that added no value — each became a redesign target.

As-is

  1. Customer takes a ticket and waits in a single queue
  2. Teller verifies identity manually at the counter
  3. Customer re-queues for a second transaction type
  4. Paper receipt filed manually end-of-day

To-be

  1. Pre-registration kiosk sorts customers by need on arrival
  2. Single-visit handling — one staff member, one counter
  3. Routine transactions guided to assisted digital stations
  4. Digital receipts auto-filed; staff freed for advisory work

4 · Recommendations

  • Pilot in 4 branches first — validate the kiosk triage and single-visit flow before network rollout; measure wait time weekly.
  • Retrain, don't reduce — redeploy freed teller hours to advisory and onboarding conversations, protecting morale and service quality.
  • Track the migration funnel — weekly dashboard on digital-shift rate per branch; coach the bottom quartile with playbooks from top performers.
  • Close the loop with customers — post-visit SMS survey for the first 90 days to confirm satisfaction actually follows the time savings.
